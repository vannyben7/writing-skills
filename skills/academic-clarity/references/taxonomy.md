# D1–D14: contextual taxonomy

Keep the original code meanings for comparison with earlier audits. A phrase can be valid in one context and redundant in another. Use the decision and preservation tests in `SKILL.md` before acting. Multiple codes may apply; choose the ones that explain the issue rather than labeling every word.

| Code | Pattern and possible cues | Useful action | False-positive guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Reviewer-facing prebuttal: “we emphasize,” “to avoid misunderstanding”; “需要强调的是”“为免误解” | Tighten the wrapper or reframe around the stated fact. | A real conceptual ambiguity or documented criticism may require explicit clarification. Do not invent the critic's motives. |
| D2 | Repeated non-claim disclaimers: “we do not claim”; “本文并不主张” | Combine duplicative statements while retaining the excluded inference. | “Not representative” and “not causal” are distinct limits. A positive description of the sample does not replace either. |
| D3 | Caveat stacking: several hedges and restrictions around a claim; “可能”“初步”“仅限于” clustered together | Remove repetition only after identifying the function of each qualifier. | Retain independent limits and uncertainty. There is no one-caveat rule. |
| D4 | Excusing imperfect results: “still encouraging given the difficulty”; “考虑到任务难度，仍令人鼓舞” | Bring the reported result into view; separate evidence from an explanatory hypothesis. | Negative results and supported explanations are information. A tentative explanation must remain tentative if retained. |
| D5 | Justifying omitted experiments or sources: “unnecessary,” “unfair comparison”; “无需比较”“不具可比性” | State a material omission and the documented reason plainly. | Do not erase non-comparison, access constraints, selection criteria, or a reproducibility-relevant decision. Never invent another method's assumptions. |
| D6 | Fairness self-defense: “to ensure fairness”; “为保证公平” | Replace a general fairness assertion with existing control details when those details express its full meaning. | Fairness may be the studied ethical or statistical property. Preserve its definition and result; identical settings alone do not prove fairness. |
| D7 | Repeated preliminary or limited-scope labels: “exploratory,” “proof of concept”; “初步”“探索性” | Reduce repeated labeling of the same status. | Exploratory and confirmatory analyses differ; a feasibility result is not an effectiveness result. Keep status where needed. |
| D8 | Legalistic disclaimer prose: long lists of excluded interpretations | Express the same necessary distinctions compactly. | Ethical, legal, consent, provenance, and institutional disclosures may be required. Their apparent formality is not grounds for removal. |
| D9 | Irrelevant defensive disclosure: process detours or failed-attempt narration | Tighten or relocate genuinely incidental chronology within the authorized scope. | Retain protocol deviations, analytic choices, failed replications, excluded observations, negative cases, and other details affecting validity or reproducibility. If relevance is unclear, keep or query. |
| D10 | Promotional compensation: “robust,” “reliable,” “safe,” “comprehensive”; “可靠”“全面”“卓越” used without an explicit basis | Remove empty praise or use measurements already supplied and linked to the same property. | These words may name a technical construct or supported finding. Do not invent statistics, substitute accuracy for safety, or silently demote an asserted empirical property. Query missing support. |
| D11 | Automatic summary sentences: “taken together”; “综上所述”“由此可见” followed by repetition | Cut duplicated framing or make an existing synthesis easier to identify. | A synthesis, inferential step, contrast resolution, or course-essay thesis may add analytical content. Preserve it and its certainty. A transition is not evidence of AI authorship. |
| D12 | Evidence-boundary over-signaling: recurrent “within the evaluated scope” / “在本研究范围内” | Tighten a repeated boundary if the same inference remains clear to the local reader. | Keep boundaries attached to source-dependent and causal claims. Abstracts and captions may require their own concise limits. |
| D13 | Absolute defensive claims: “any method would fail,” “guarantees”; “必然”“绝不可能” | Check the exact stated condition; flag an unsupported absolute without supplying a new theorem or premise. | A demonstrated guarantee, logically necessary condition, or precise universal statement should not be weakened just to sound modest. |
| D14 | Contribution-by-relabeling: novelty defended by a new term; “并非简单套用”“全新范式” | State the actual distinction already present in the text. | Technical terminology, a defended interpretation, and a bounded novelty claim may be substantive. Do not adjudicate novelty or invent a technical contribution. |

The original `NECESSARY_CAVEAT`, `DEFENSIVE`, `MIXED`, and `CLEAN` labels remain available for continuity, but do not replace a reasoned disposition. If priorities are useful, describe the effect on interpretation: a lost causal boundary matters more than a redundant introduction phrase. Do not equate formal or cautious prose with severe writing failure.
